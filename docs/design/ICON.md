# crank app icon — v1

Issue: [#48](https://github.com/gerchowl/crank/issues/48). Date: 2026-10-13.

## Chosen concept — `/C/` crankset mark

A bold geometric **C** (opening facing right) with a **crankset at its centre**:
a chainring hub, and two crank arms 180° apart on one axis through the hub.
The upper-right arm exits cleanly through the C's opening and terminates in a
pedal-spindle dot. The opposite arm points to the lower-left and merges into
the body of the C's lower stroke.

### Why

- **Name-matched.** The `C` is for *crank*; the mark reads as the letter
  without being a wordmark. The crankset at its centre makes it mechanical,
  not typographic.
- **First-principles cycling, not a bike cliché.** Two arms 180° apart on a
  shared axis is literally how a bike crankset is built. There is no bike,
  no wheel, no gear teeth, no cartoon rider, no lightning bolt, no
  speedometer dial — all of which the owner ruled out.
- **AI coach ↔ precision.** The symmetric arm axis + the pedal dot read as
  measured geometry (position, torque around an axis). It is not an activity
  ring and not a Zwift-orange splash.
- **Icon fundamentals.** One bold silhouette, two colours, no text, holds
  shape at 16 px, centred on the squircle grid with standard margin.

### Final geometry (round 2)

- Canvas: 1024 × 1024, iOS Big Sur+ squircle (rounded square, corner radius
  230, filling the canvas; corners outside the squircle are transparent)
- C ring: outer radius 350, stroke 125, gap half-angle 62° (opening ≈ 124°
  on the right, generous enough that the steep crank arms clear the C's
  stroke cleanly), butt caps
- Arm axis: **50° above horizontal**; upper arm length 420, lower arm
  length 310 (ends inside the C's stroke body)
- Crank arms: tapered polygons, **wide at the hub (width 118) narrowing to
  the pedal end (width 78)** with a rounded pedal cap. Arm/C-stroke ratio
  at the hub ≈ 0.94. The taper reads as a real forged crank arm, not a
  thin gauge needle.
- Chainring: filled brass **annulus** (outer radius 108, inner BB-hole
  radius 56). The annulus — a brass ring around a dark central hole where
  the bottom-bracket spindle sits — is unmistakably a chainring, not a
  simple hub.
- Pedal spindle dot: dark background-coloured dot (radius 26) at the tip
  of the upper arm.

### Why these numbers (round 2)

Round 1 shipped a 30° axis with thin uniform arms. Architect review
caught two defects both visible in that version's own previews:

1. **At 16/32 px the mark read as a lowercase "e".** The 30° axis is
   close enough to horizontal that the arm became the e's crossbar.
2. **Thin uniform arms + a small hub read as a gauge needle, not a
   crankset.**

Round 2 fixes both:

- **50° axis** (vs 30°): the arm is now visibly a diagonal, not a
  near-horizontal crossbar. Side-by-side with a reference lowercase "e"
  rendered at the same sizes/weight (`etest.svg`, SF Pro black at
  900 pt), the mark no longer reads as a letter — the right-side gap
  stays open, the diagonal is clearly diagonal, and the chainring hub in
  the middle has no analogue in an "e".
- **Tapered arms** (hub 118 → pedal 78), with the hub width roughly 94 %
  of the C stroke: now reads as a forged crank arm, not a needle.
- **Chainring annulus** (not just a hub disc): a brass ring with a
  visible central BB hole makes "crankset" explicit — this is the one
  component in cycling whose silhouette is a ring with crank arms on it.

### Palette (two colours, no gradient)

| role              | hex       | notes                                |
| ----------------- | --------- | ------------------------------------ |
| background        | `#1a1d22` | dark graphite near-black             |
| mark (brass)      | `#c08a4a` | warm muted burnished brass           |

Deliberately avoids Zwift's orange-red (`#fc6719`-ish) and TrainerRoad's
red (`#e32d20`-ish). Graphite + brass reads as an instrument, not a toy.

## Process

1. Four concepts drafted (C1–C4), then a fifth (C5) attempted mid-process.
2. Each concept generated with ribes/FLUX on sage; C1 iterated through v2–v7.
3. **Direction change** mid-session from the owner: switch primary concept
   to a hand-authored `/C/` crankset vector. Flux concepts kept only as
   comparison; the final icon is **not** generated by diffusion.
4. Vector variants built as SVG (`make_svg.py`), rendered with
   `rsvg-convert` (nixpkgs#librsvg 2.60.0), reviewed at 1024/256/128/64/32/16 px.

The reproducible pattern: flux's priors for "bicycle crank" strongly
include gear teeth and chainrings, and its priors for "icon" strongly
include an iOS-7-style long shadow. Even heavy negatives did not fully
suppress these. For a precise geometric mark, a hand-authored vector is
the right tool.

### Small-size review (round 2) — "e" test

Rendered a reference lowercase "e" in SF Pro Display Black at 900 pt on
the same squircle (`etest.svg`), then rendered at 16 / 32 / 64 px and
compared against the chosen mark at the same sizes with the `Read` tool.

- **Reference "e"** at those sizes: closed oval top loop, horizontal
  crossbar through the middle, visible brass-on-dark letter.
- **Chosen mark** at 16 / 32 / 64 px:
  - **64 px**: fully readable crankset — C-with-gap on the right, chainring
    annulus (brass ring + dark BB hole) in the centre, two tapered arms
    at 50°, pedal spindle dot at the upper tip.
  - **32 px**: C's right-side gap is still visible as a dark wedge, the
    arm is clearly a diagonal (not a crossbar), the chainring hub reads
    as a bright-with-dark-centre blob mid-mark. Does **not** read as "e":
    the diagonal, the gap, and the dark-centred hub are all signals that
    an "e" at the same size does not carry.
  - **16 px**: reduces to "a C with a diagonal slash and a bright centre
    pip". Still C-shaped; the diagonal defeats the "e" crossbar reading.
    Pedal dot is lost to subpixel noise (expected at this size).

How I judged "not an e":
1. the brass is **open** on the right (dark gap visible) vs the e's
   closed top loop;
2. the internal line is a **diagonal**, not a horizontal crossbar;
3. the centre carries a dark-cored blob (the chainring BB hole) that
   an e does not have.

## Provenance

- Flux concept studies: generated via the `ribes` MCP's local FLUX worker
  (`backend: "flux"`) running on `sage` (fleet GPU host). Model and sampler
  as configured by that service. See `~/.local/share/ribes/images/` for the
  raw outputs; thread names are listed in the iterations table above.
- Final icon: **hand-authored SVG**, rasterised with `rsvg-convert`
  (nixpkgs#librsvg 2.60.0). The flux outputs are not shipped — they only
  informed the direction before the owner's `/C/` concept arrived.

## Known limits and follow-ups

- The master PNG is a pre-shaped squircle with transparent corners.
  `tauri icon` (planned in the scaffold spike #28) should consume
  `assets/icon/icon-1024.png` and generate `.icns`/`.ico`/`AppIcon.iconset`
  from it. **To verify in #28:** confirm Tauri does not re-apply its own
  squircle mask on top of ours (which would produce a double-rounded corner).
  If it does, a full-bleed square source may be required instead.
- At 16 px the pedal-spindle dot disappears. If a cleaner menu-bar
  silhouette is wanted, a dedicated 16 px variant (dot removed, strokes
  thickened by ~10%) can be optimised later.
- The icon is intentionally dark-first (deep graphite); it will contrast
  well in both Light Mode and Dark Mode Dock, but on a very dark wallpaper
  the squircle outline becomes subtle. Acceptable.
